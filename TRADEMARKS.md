## You may, without asking

- Use a project's name to refer to the project truthfully: in articles, talks, teaching, papers, package

- Say that your work "is based on", "uses" or "is compatible with" a project, when that is true.
- Use a logo, unchanged, to refer to or link to the project: in talks, articles, teaching, package listings,
  badges and websites. You may scale it, and you may use the colour variants we publish.
- Use the names and logos for a free community event or meetup about the project.

## Please don't

- Use our names or logos in a way that suggests we made, approve, certify or support your product, service,
  fork or event when we don't. Don't call your version "official".
- Change the logos, or combine them with your own marks.
- Use a logo as the brand or icon of your own product, service or app, or make it larger or more prominent
  than your own name.
- Use our names, or names easily confused with them, as the name of your fork, product, company, domain,
  social media handle, or package on a public registry.
- Put our logos on merchandise you sell.

For any other use, ask us first.

## Forks

If you distribute a modified version of a project:
- give it its own name and logo, and don't use ours as its brand;
- keep the LICENSE and NOTICE files and the copyright notices, as the Apache licence requires;
- you may say truthfully where it came from: "based on <project name>, by African River Corridors".

You don't need to rename code identifiers, file formats or internal module names. A fork kept only to prepare
changes for us is fine under the project's name.

## Engineering outputs

Our engineering tools mark their outputs "screening — not for construction". If you share outputs under our
name or a project's name, keep that wording. Don't present those outputs as certified, approved or class-ready
by us.

## Marking

Where practical, mark the first prominent use of a project name or logo with ™. Don't use ®: our marks are
not registered.
